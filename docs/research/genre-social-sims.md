# Social-World / Decoration Game Genre Survey

Research pass across 11 social-simulation / avatar-decoration games, extracting the mechanics that drove engagement, retention, and eventual decline. Sourced via web search; claims without a clean primary source are flagged [UNVERIFIED].

---

## YoVille (2008, Tall Tree Games → Zynga; later YoWorld, Big Viking Games)

**Currency.** Two-tier currency split, the template later reused across Zynga's "-Ville" line: YoCoins earned daily via simple timed jobs (e.g. working the in-game factory), and YoCash as the premium currency bought with real money (roughly 25 YoCash per $5, or 50,000 YoCoins per $1). ([YoWorld Wiki](https://yoworld.fandom.com/wiki/YoVille.com), [Positioniseverything](https://www.positioniseverything.net/updated-zynga-to-finally-shut-down-one-of-its-oldest-games-yoville/))

**Decoration/customization.** Players owned a virtual apartment decorated with furniture bought via coins, plus avatar clothing. Room decoration contests ran as early as 2009, with the top-voted room winning free currency and a free home — an explicit status-display incentive layered onto decorating. ([YoWorld Wiki](https://yoworld.fandom.com/wiki/YoVille.com))

**Social.** Core loop: apartment to decorate, avatar to customize, rooms to visit, public spaces for real-time chat with strangers and friends. Players could enter friends' apartments directly from a buddy list. Gifting launched in February 2009 — send clothing/furniture to another player *without requiring their consent or presence in the room*, which is a low-friction, high-frequency social touch. ([Virtual Worlds Museum](https://www.virtualworlds.museum/exhibits/yoville), [Gifting — YoWorld Wiki](https://yoworld.fandom.com/wiki/Gifting))

**Retention hooks.** Daily jobs (timed minigames) for coin income; the apartment/avatar as a persistent, visitable status display functions as the retention anchor rather than a hard energy-timer.

**Fate.** Grew to 4M+ monthly Facebook active users by early 2009, briefly surpassing WoW's North American population. ([Virtual Worlds Museum](https://www.virtualworlds.museum/exhibits/yoville)) Currency could be purchased in bulk and counterfeit/exploited currency caused economic inflation, splitting the player base into "super-rich" and "super-poor" and undermining the earn→spend loop for new players. Zynga shut it down January 10, 2014; Tall Tree/Big Viking Games bought it back and relaunched it as YoWorld in April 2014. ([YoWorld Wiki](https://yoworld.fandom.com/wiki/YoVille.com), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YoWorld))

## FarmVille (2009, Zynga)

**Currency.** Coins (earned via harvesting) and Farm Cash (premium, bought with real money), spent on seeds, decorations, animals, and buildings.

**Decoration/customization.** The farm itself is the decorated space — crop layout, buildings, animals, and cosmetic decorations are arranged and shown off, functioning the same status-display role that a room does in YoVille/Pet Society.

**Social.** Deeply wired into the Facebook social graph: players invited friends to join and could request/give resources (fertilizer, building materials) from and to friends, driving the game's news-feed virality. ([FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/what-happened-to-farmville/))

**Retention hooks — the genre's most-cited case study.**
- *Appointment dynamics*: crops mature on a timer and must be harvested within a window or they wither, discarding the player's investment. This is explicit loss-aversion design — the fear of losing a planted crop is a stronger motivator than the reward of a fresh one. ([Textuality.org](http://www.textuality.org/2011/11/farmville-and-glitch-exogenous-and-endogenous-game-mechanics.html))
- *Consecutive-login rewards* that improve with streak length, reinforcing the habit loop. ([Grant's Games](https://grantsgames.com/tag/retention/))
- *Buildables/collections*: players assemble a final item (house, decoration) from parts either gathered from friends over time or bought outright — and Zynga's own data showed players would not pay merely to progress a collection, only to *complete* one, a strong "so close" monetization lever. ([Lewis, Wardrip-Fruin, FDG 2012 paper](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/~ejw/papers/lewis-motivational-game-design-patterns-fdg2012.pdf))

**Fate.** Peaked around 84 million monthly users in March 2010, Facebook's most popular game for two-plus years. ([FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/what-happened-to-farmville/)) The same virality mechanic (friend-request spam, excessive news-feed posts) drew user complaints, and Facebook throttled game invites and feed permissions in response, choking off new-user acquisition. A former Zynga chief designer admitted: "We optimized for virality over fun. In retrospect, that short-term thinking hurt us. Players felt used, not entertained." ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/farmville-social-gaming-and-addiction), [Mervyn Chua](https://mervynchua.com/when-virality-fades-what-zyngas-decline-teaches-us-about-innovation-human-centered-design-growth/)) The original Facebook FarmVille shut down at end of 2020, officially attributed to Adobe discontinuing Flash. ([Distractify](https://www.distractify.com/p/why-is-farmville-shutting-down))

## Pet Society (2009, Playfish → EA)

**Currency.** Gold coins, earned primarily through pet-care actions — feeding, washing, brushing, playing — and notably by performing these actions on *friends'* pets during visits (20-50 coins per interaction), directly tying income to socializing. Playfish Cash was the premium currency, required for exclusive items and some areas. ([Pet Society Wiki](https://petpedia.fandom.com/wiki/Pet_Society))

**Decoration/customization.** Players designed pets (gender, name, color, appearance) and decorated an expanding multi-room house with purchased furniture; house size grew as the player leveled up via "Paw Points." ([Virtual Worlds Museum](https://www.virtualworlds.museum/exhibits/pet-society))

**Social.** Visiting friends' pets was the core loop, not a side feature — care actions on a friend's pet paid out coins directly. A distinctive viral gifting mechanic: finding "Hideeni" (a hidden costumed character) let a player post a surprise gift to their Facebook news feed, claimable by the first five friends who clicked it — a scarcity-driven, feed-visible viral hook distinct from FarmVille's request-spam approach. Party Invitations and Surprise Birthday Boxes extended this into event-based gifting. ([Pet Society Wiki](https://petpedia.fandom.com/wiki/Pet_Society), [AOL](https://www.aol.com/news/2010-08-03-pet-society-turns-two-throw-a-shindig-with-the-new-party-invita.html))

**Retention hooks.** Pet neediness (feeding/care) functions as a soft appointment mechanic; house/room unlocks via leveling give a long-tail progression hook.

**Fate.** Peaked at 50 million monthly players and 5 million daily players, reportedly earning up to $100,000/day in item sales. ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/us/Facebook-Social-Game-Sim-City,news-16947.html)) EA shut it down June 2013 alongside The Sims Social and SimCity Social, citing declining activity — the broader Facebook-canvas-game collapse, not a Pet Society-specific failure. ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/us/Facebook-Social-Game-Sim-City,news-16947.html))

## Club Penguin (2005, launched by New Horizon Interactive; acquired by Disney 2007)

**Currency.** Coins earned by playing mini-games, spent on clothing and igloo furniture. Membership (paid subscription) gated larger igloos and more customization. ([Virtual Worlds Wiki](https://virtual-worlds.fandom.com/wiki/Club_Penguin))

**Decoration/customization.** Igloo decoration with purchased items; puffles (Tamagotchi-like pets) added a care-and-collect layer distinct from decoration. ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91518708/meet-the-club-penguin-superfans-giving-the-game-a-second-life))

**Social.** A Buddy/Friends List (up to 500 friends) for tracking who's online; a heavily moderated "Safe Chat" system (preset phrases, later "Ultimate Safe Chat" with freer typed text under filtering) that let it operate as a chat platform for children while managing safety risk — by 2011 the game logged 56 million chat messages daily. At least one themed party per month (Halloween, Holiday, Music Jam, Puffle Party, Medieval Party, etc.), usually with a free exclusive item, gave a recurring event cadence. ([Club Penguin Wiki](https://clubpenguin.fandom.com/wiki/Friends_List), [Virtual Worlds Wiki](https://virtual-worlds.fandom.com/wiki/Club_Penguin))

**Retention hooks.** Monthly parties as an appointment mechanic (limited-time free items create FOMO); puffle care as a secondary daily-return hook; membership subscription as a recurring revenue/retention combo.

**Fate.** Sold to Disney in 2007 for a reported $700 million valuation at over 12 million users; grew to 200 million+ registered accounts by July 2013. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Penguin), [Instagram/Disney citation](https://www.instagram.com/p/DPjlplAkV_n/)) Disney shut down the original servers March 29, 2017, citing insufficient revenue to justify server costs, replacing it with a mobile-only "Club Penguin Island" that itself later shut down. A farewell "Waddle On Party" ran the two months prior. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/01/31/club-penguin-is-shutting-down/?ncid=rss)) Notably, the game has an unusual afterlife: adult former players run private-server revivals (Club Penguin Rewritten and others), one of which Disney had shut down over copyright infringement, with arrests made. ([Sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/esports/club-penguin-rewritten-shuts-down-survivors-remain), [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91518708/meet-the-club-penguin-superfans-giving-the-game-a-second-life))

## Webkinz (2005, Ganz)

**Currency.** KinzCash, earned by adopting new pets (each physical toy's code grants an allotment), playing minigames, answering trivia, and daily rituals: clicking "I Love My Webkinz!", spinning the Wheel of WOW, playing Wishing Well 2, and "jobs" (minigames available once every 8 hours). ([Webkinz Wiki](https://virtual-worlds.fandom.com/wiki/Webkinz))

**Decoration/customization.** Room building — placing furniture, expanding the house, decorating themed rooms — plus pet care (feeding, washing, playing) tied to a happiness/health leveling system. Newer "Webkinz Next" adds direct one-for-one player trading with color-coded rarity/fairness indicators. ([Webkinz Wiki](https://virtual-worlds.fandom.com/wiki/Webkinz))

**Social.** Weaker than most of this list — Webkinz's social layer is thinner than Club Penguin's or YoVille's; trading is present but the game is not built around visiting other players' rooms as the core loop. [UNVERIFIED — could not confirm a room-visiting/friend-list feature comparable to Club Penguin's in the sources gathered.]

**Retention hooks.** The physical toy is the retention hook: KinzCash access and even continued account relevance are gated behind buying more toys (each with a code), which is a hardware-to-software retention bridge unique among these games. The 8-hour job cooldown and daily click rituals (Wheel of WOW, Wishing Well) are appointment mechanics.

**Fate.** Peaked around 2008-2009 (1M+ users, 3.2M visitors on Easter 2009 alone), then fell off a cliff — physical toy sales down 41% by August 2009. ([Michigan Daily](https://www.michigandaily.com/arts/digital-culture/an-elegy-to-webkinz-20-years-old-today/)) Its tie to a physical toy purchase, which drove the initial hook, became its ceiling: unlike browser-only competitors, continued engagement required continued toy purchases, and it never had Neopets' resurgence during the pandemic-era nostalgia wave. The genre-wide decline of virtual pets in the 2010s compounded this. ([Gamers with Glasses](https://www.gamerswithglasses.com/features/virtual-pets-rise-and-fall))

## The Sims Online (2002, EA/Maxis; rebranded EA-Land 2007-2008)

**Currency.** Simoleons, earned through jobs; skill training (mechanical, cooking, charisma, body, creativity, logic) gated better jobs and higher pay, spent on real estate, furniture, and clothing. ([Sims Wiki](https://sims.fandom.com/wiki/The_Sims_Online))

**Decoration/customization.** House building/decoration inherited from the offline Sims, but critically: EA did *not* allow user-created custom content (clothing, objects) at launch, unlike the wildly popular offline Sims modding scene — only added at the EA-Land rebrand in 2007-2008, by which point it was "too little, too late." ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/shocking-things-you-didnt-know-about-the-miserable-sims-online/))

**Social.** Persistent shared world with real players controlling every Sim, roommates sharing houses, and object-based group interactions.

**Retention hooks.** Job/skill progression as a grind loop, but the "gameplay" was largely passive — click an object, then watch and chat while an animation plays out, converting what should be idle social time into a "waste" relative to the game's stated money-earning goal. ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/shocking-things-you-didnt-know-about-the-miserable-sims-online/))

**Fate.** A cautionary tale, not a success story. $20M budget against only 125,000 retail copies sold and 97,000 active subscribers six months post-launch. ([Escapist Magazine](https://www.escapistmagazine.com/20-million-dollar-failure/)) Core failure: the economy made earning money the only success path, so casual socializing (the genre's actual draw) was disincentivized as unproductive, and the lack of user-generated content at launch removed the creative-expression hook that made offline Sims sticky. Rebranded EA-Land in March 2007; shut down entirely August 1, 2008. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sims_Online))

## Gaia Online (2003, Gaia Interactive)

**Currency.** Gold, earned by playing minigames and posting in forums, plus a daily "Daily Chance" random gold/item reward. Later added a "platinum" currency (10 million gold per platinum) after a hyperinflation crisis. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaia_Online), [Productmint](https://productmint.com/what-happened-to-gaia-online/))

**Decoration/customization.** Avatar customization (skin tone, eyes, hair, gender, attire) is the primary "space" — Gaia is avatar-first rather than room-first, with items ranging from normal clothing to fantastical accessories (wings, tails, halos). A virtual home existed but avatar display was the dominant status signal, closely coupled to the forums where the avatar is your public identity. ([Gaiapedia](https://gaia.fandom.com/wiki/Avatar))

**Social.** Built on forums and guilds (hangout, roleplay, topic-based) rather than a graphical visiting mechanic — social interaction and avatar display are fused: your avatar appears next to every forum post, making item rarity a persistent status broadcast to the entire community, not just visitors to your space. Player-to-player item marketplace supplemented site-run shops. ([Gaia Fandom](https://gaia.fandom.com/wiki/Go-Gaia/About))

**Retention hooks.** Daily Chance login reward; forum posting itself earned currency, tying the social/discussion loop directly to the earn loop (distinct from every other game surveyed, where earning is a separate minigame layer).

**Fate.** Peaked around 2007 at 7 million unique monthly visitors. ([Productmint](https://productmint.com/what-happened-to-gaia-online/)) A severe, self-inflicted **hyperinflation crisis**: a new CEO/COO-era "gold generator" item sold for real money, generated large amounts of gold, and drove item prices into the hundreds of billions of gold within a year or two, destroying the currency's meaning. Gold generator sales were scrapped in February 2017, and a stabilizing "platinum" currency introduced in August 2017. ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/events/hyperinflation-in-gaia-online), [Productmint](https://productmint.com/what-happened-to-gaia-online/)) Combined with forums declining as a communication format generally, the site never recovered its peak audience but remains operational with a much smaller (reportedly low-thousands daily) active base as of the most recent reporting found. ([Productmint](https://productmint.com/what-happened-to-gaia-online/))

## IMVU (2004, founded by Will Harvey and Eric Ries)

**Currency.** IMVU Credits, exchange rate roughly $1 = 1000 Credits, spent on avatar clothing, furniture, and rooms from a catalog of 50+ million user-made items (as of 2025). ([Livecards](https://livecards.net/en/all-blogs/imvu-your-3d-avatar-social-world), [MiaKasCreations](https://www.miakascreations.com/index.php/blog/37-what-is-imvu-and-how-to-content-creators-make-money))

**Decoration/customization — the standout feature of this game.** IMVU's "Creator" program lets users design and sell original 3D avatar items and rooms into the catalog for Credits, which convert back to real money — genuine user-generated-content commerce, not merely arranging pre-made assets. More than 50,000 people reportedly earn money this way. ([IMVU Official Medium](https://medium.com/imvu-official/creativity-is-currency-in-the-participation-economy-at-imvu-8774bf03e969))

**Social.** 3D avatar chat rooms as the core social space; company origin is notable — IMVU's early product pivots (via rapid, metrics-driven customer interviews) became the founding case study for Eric Ries's *Lean Startup* methodology. ([Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/imvu-history-eric-ries-lean-startup/))

**Retention hooks.** Creator monetization itself is a retention hook distinct from the rest of this list — creators return to maintain a storefront/income, not just to play.

**Fate.** Still operating, decades on — the longest-lived platform on this list alongside Gaia and Webkinz. Faced a 2015 class-action settlement over shortened-playback audio files sold as "full length." ([Top Class Actions](https://topclassactions.com/lawsuit-settlements/closed-settlements/imvu-audio-file-class-action-settlement/)) More recently (2025), community reports describe age-verification gaps letting minors reach the platform's adult "Access Pass" content, and creator/VIP subscriber dissatisfaction over content moderation. [UNVERIFIED as an official cause of decline — these are community-forum complaints, not confirmed churn data.] ([IMVU Community Center](https://support.imvu.com/support/discussions/topics/154001069364))

## Highrise (2018, Pocket Worlds)

**Currency.** Gold as the earned/premium currency; free-to-play with in-app purchases, plus daily activities, quests, and seasonal contests for earning. ([Highrise app listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/highrise-dress-up-avatar-game/id924589795))

**Decoration/customization.** Deep avatar customization (hair, features, clothing, accessories, body modification) is the headline feature, marketed explicitly around "player creation." Public hubs, private rooms, clubs, and themed lounges serve different social contexts (casual mingling vs. reserved roleplay/tournament space). ([Highrise.game](https://highrise.game/), [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/games/highrise-passes-250m-revenue-milestone-by-empowering-player-creation/))

**Social.** A player-run secondary market for rare outfits/"fits," reportedly worth millions in aggregate trade value — closer to Highrise functioning as a fashion-NFT-adjacent economy than a simple decorate-and-chat loop. A feed layer mimicking Instagram (posts, selfies, videos) sits alongside the room-based chat. ([Highrise app listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/highrise-dress-up-avatar-game/id924589795))

**Retention hooks.** Daily activities and short quests, seasonal contests — standard Zynga-style appointment design, adapted for mobile.

**Fate.** Reported to have passed $250 million in cumulative marketplace revenue as of June 2025, across 10M+ users (Pocket Worlds cites 40M+ across its portfolio). ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/games/highrise-passes-250m-revenue-milestone-by-empowering-player-creation/), [Boringbusinessnerd](https://www.boringbusinessnerd.com/startups/pocket-worlds)) I could not confirm specific 2022-2023 user decline or layoffs at Pocket Worlds/Highrise — the metaverse-industry-wide contraction reporting from that period (Meta Reality Labs layoffs, Horizon Worlds' weak retention) is well documented, but Highrise-specific figures for the same period were not found. [UNVERIFIED for Highrise specifically.]

## Everskies (2022, launched by a small team; grown to 20M+ players)

**Currency.** Stardust, earned through built-in minigames, plus a "Stars" earn-currency layer through third-party offer/app integrations. ([App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/everskies-avatar-dress-up/id1604095327))

**Decoration/customization.** Avatar-and-outfit "aesthetic" matching across many named subcultures (Y2K, Lolita, anime, "lazy style," etc.) is the core creative act. Users can design original wearable/DIY items for sale in player-run stores, not just arrange pre-made catalog pieces — closer to IMVU's creator model than to FarmVille's pure-purchase model. ([App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/everskies-avatar-dress-up/id1604095327))

**Social.** Chat, clubs, competitions, and a trade-offer system for exchanging rare fashion items — collecting/trading rare looks functions as the status-display and social-validation layer.

**Retention hooks.** Trading and competitions provide a recurring reason to check in; specific streak/energy mechanics not confirmed in sources gathered. [UNVERIFIED.]

**Fate.** Currently active and growing (20M+ players cited); no shutdown or confirmed decline data found. Represents the modern "TikTok-aesthetic" evolution of Gaia Online's avatar-display-as-identity model, but built for mobile-native Gen Z fashion subcultures rather than forums.

## Pixel Worlds (2017, Kukouri Mobile Entertainment)

**Currency.** Multiple layered currencies: Gems (earned via farming, fishing, selling items, tournament wins) as the backbone, plus Byte Coins (introduced 2018) as a global trading currency usable at a dedicated "PWE Terminal" or in direct player trades. ([Pixel Worlds Wiki](https://pixelworlds.fandom.com/wiki/Byte_Coins))

**Decoration/customization.** A true sandbox: players build anything from parkour courses to pixel art to entire trading-hub "worlds," not just a single room — closer to Growtopia (which it is widely compared to/inspired by) than to the single-apartment model of YoVille or Pet Society. ([Game Solver](https://game-solver.com/pixel-worlds-mmo-sandbox/))

**Social.** 100% player-driven economy — item and even *world* values are set entirely by what players will pay, with no fixed shop prices for player-made goods; trading hubs and shops are themselves player-built spaces. Farming/crossbreeding of trees to yield new blocks and clothing items adds an FarmVille-esque collect/combine loop inside a sandbox-building game. ([Steam Community Guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3221116015), [arXiv: Market Interventions in a Large-Scale Virtual Economy](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.07970))

**Retention hooks.** Weekly fishing tournaments awarding gems; farming/crossbreeding timers as a FarmVille-style appointment mechanic layered onto the sandbox.

**Fate.** Won "Best Indie Game Developer" at the Global Mobile Game Awards 2019, still updated as of mid-2023. ([App listing](https://www.pgyer.com/apk/apk/com.kukouri.wizworld)) Community reports describe it as "slowly dying," attributed to lack of marketing/ad spend rather than a design failure — i.e., a discovery problem, not a retention problem. [Community forum claim, treat as UNVERIFIED for cause though the "declining" state itself is corroborated by multiple community posts.] ([Steam Community Guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3221116015))

## Cross-Game Synthesis

**The universal loop.** Every game in this survey runs the same core cycle: **earn currency (minigame/job/care-action) → spend on decoration or avatar items → display to other players (room visit, forum avatar, feed post) → get social validation or triggers a friend's reciprocal action → return to earn more.** The games differ mainly in *where* the display happens (a private room in YoVille/Pet Society/Club Penguin/Webkinz; the avatar itself in Gaia/Highrise/Everskies/IMVU; a built structure in Pixel Worlds/The Sims Online) and *how tightly earning is coupled to socializing* — Gaia and Pet Society pay you directly for social actions (forum posts, caring for a friend's pet); FarmVille and YoVille pay you for solo minigames and treat social contact as a separate request/gift layer.

**Retention mechanics that recur almost everywhere.** (1) A withering/decaying asset that punishes absence via loss aversion — FarmVille's crops, Webkinz's pet happiness, Pet Society's pet neediness. (2) Daily login rituals or streak-scaling rewards — Webkinz's Wheel of WOW and 8-hour job cooldown, Gaia's Daily Chance, Highrise's daily quests. (3) Scheduled events creating artificial scarcity — Club Penguin's monthly parties, Pet Society's Hideeni gift drops, Pixel Worlds' weekly tournaments. (4) Collection/"buildable" completion bias — Zynga's own data (FDG 2012 paper) found players won't pay to merely progress a collection, only to finish it, which is why nearly every game in this list has some assemble-the-set mechanic.

**What players remember fondly vs. resent.** Fondly: gifting (YoVille, Pet Society's surprise-gift drops), visiting friends' spaces, dress-up/room-decoration as creative expression, and — for the minority of games that supported it — genuine user-made content (IMVU, Everskies, Pixel Worlds' player-built worlds). Resented, per the sources gathered: FarmVille's and the broader Zynga "Ville" catalog's friend-request spam and forced virality, which Facebook itself throttled and which Zynga's own chief designer later called optimizing "virality over fun" at the cost of player trust; Gaia's and YoVille's currency hyperinflation from exploits/pay-to-win generators, which hollowed out the earn→spend loop for anyone not already wealthy in-game; and the pure grind-for-money design of The Sims Online, which converted idle social time into something the game framed as "wasted."

**User-generated content is the strongest correlate of longevity in this set.** The two oldest still-operating platforms surveyed, IMVU (2004) and Gaia Online (2003, reduced but alive), both let ordinary users *create and sell* original items/rooms, not merely arrange a fixed catalog — as does Pixel Worlds' player-built sandbox and, more recently, Everskies' player-designed DIY items. By contrast, the games built purely around arranging purchased/catalog content — YoVille, FarmVille, Pet Society, Club Penguin, The Sims Online (until its too-late EA-Land pivot) — all shut down outright, most within 5-11 years of launch, once the parent company's business priorities shifted or the underlying platform (Flash, Facebook canvas games) was deprecated. The Sims Online is the sharpest illustration: EA researchers and players agree the *absence* of the offline game's signature custom-content modding at launch was a primary failure driver, and adding it at the EA-Land rebrand came too late to matter. The implication for a new build: catalog-only decoration is a rented, platform-dependent hook; creator tools convert players into an install base the platform itself doesn't have to keep restocking.

**Open questions / gaps not resolved by this pass:**
- Webkinz's social/visiting features (room-visiting analogous to Club Penguin) could not be confirmed or denied from sources gathered — flagged [UNVERIFIED] in that section.
- Highrise's 2022-2023 user trajectory during the broader "metaverse" pullback was not found in search results specific to Pocket Worlds/Highrise (only industry-wide Meta/Reality Labs data was available).
- Everskies' retention-specific mechanics (streaks, energy systems) beyond currency-earning minigames were not confirmed.
- Pixel Worlds' claimed "dying" state is corroborated by community sentiment but not by hard player-count data; treat the *cause* (marketing/discovery vs. design fatigue) as unverified.
